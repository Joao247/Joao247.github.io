<Part title="The problem">
  <p>In the Czech Republic, sales users check a company against <strong>ARES</strong>, the national company register, before creating it as an account. The legacy CRM supported this lookup, so the new SAP Sales Cloud landscape had to keep it.</p>
  <p>The rollout was also a chance to do better: less manual data entry, authoritative company, address and registration data filled in automatically, and the national company identifier used as part of duplicate prevention.</p>
</Part>

<Part title="The workflow">
  <ol>
    <li>While creating an account in SAP Sales Cloud, the user opens the onboarding app.</li>
    <li>They search the register by company name, national ID or address.</li>
    <li>They review the matching companies and preview the one they pick.</li>
    <li>The validated company data goes back into the Sales Cloud account-creation flow.</li>
  </ol>
</Part>

<Part title="The key decision">
  <p>One requirement shaped the architecture: other sales organisations and countries were likely to use different company registers later.</p>
  <Options>
    <Option label="Option A" title="Build directly against ARES">The fastest path to the Czech go-live, but every new country would mean reworking the UI and the service around another register's API.</Option>
    <Option label="Chosen" title="A stable contract with an adapter per register" chosen>The SAPUI5 app and CAP service speak one generic contract. A provider factory picks the register adapter for the user's sales organisation, and ARES is the first implementation.</Option>
  </Options>
  <p>Adding a country means adding its provider mapping, adapter and BTP destination. The UI and service stay as they are, apart from country-specific configuration and validation where needed.</p>
</Part>

  <p>The app runs behind the BTP application router and reaches the register through a BTP destination. Errors from the register's API are mapped to clear messages in the app, so users never see a raw external-service failure.</p>
</Part>

<Part title="After onboarding">
  <p>Creating the account is only the start. Company data in the register changes, so the solution also keeps accounts consistent over time.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Discovery.</strong> A job finds Sales Cloud accounts that originated from the register and stores the link between the register company and the account.</li>
    <li><strong>Change monitoring.</strong> Scheduled CAP jobs check the register for changes to linked companies.</li>
    <li><strong>Sync or review.</strong> Depending on which system owns the data for that account, a change is either synchronised into Sales Cloud or turned into a task for a person to review.</li>
  </ul>
</Part>

<Part title="Outcome">
  <p>The solution went live in production in March 2026, replacing the lookup from the legacy CRM with one that fills in register data and keeps it current.</p>
</Part>

<Part title="What I took from it">
  <p>Put the seam where change is expected. Here that was the register, so the adapter boundary is the most important line in the design.</p>
  <p>Whether to automate a sync is a question of data ownership. When the register is not the system of record for an account, a person should decide.</p>
</Part>
